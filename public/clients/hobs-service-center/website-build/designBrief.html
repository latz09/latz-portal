Project Snapshot

The job

Hob's Service Center has spent 40+ years and built a 4.9★, 91-review reputation servicing boats, ATVs, UTVs, and docks around Tomahawk and the Northwoods — and right now, none of it shows up anywhere online. Their old site lived on HIBU's platform, which quietly unpublished it; nothing is recoverable or portable, even if HIBU brings it back. This is a full rebuild, not a migration — using the one surviving Wayback capture and the client's existing logo as a loose starting point, not a locked design. Winning looks like: hobsservice.com live again, representing what the shop actually does (not old snowmobile-heavy boilerplate), owned outright on a platform that can't vanish on them again.

The Build
Scope5-page custom rebuild — Home, Services, About Us, Contact, Blog
CMSCustom-built — services, about content, photos, hours, and blog posts all self-editable
StackNext.js, React, Tailwind, Framer Motion, Vercel
Timeline~4–6 weeks from kickoff — Home copy already largely drafted from the old live content, a head start
Not in Scope (For Now)
EcommerceNone — no store, no booking/scheduler integration. This is a lead-generation site.
Service URLsEach service lives as an anchored section on one Services page, not its own URL — an accepted SEO trade-off
Brand ExtensionA fuller icon/pattern set built from the logo is a possible next step, not part of this round
PhotographyNo professional shoot — working with what the client sends, stock fills any gaps at launch

Open Design Questions
How far into the logo's yellow/black/gray workshop palette do we lean?
The existing mark already carries a strong mechanical identity. Worth deciding together how much of that carries through the full site versus softening it for readability and warmth.
Blog post format — still being worked out, hold off on finalizing
Terra sent over the first real post, but she mentioned prepping it as a straight copy/paste — not yet clear that'll translate cleanly into a structured layout (headings, photo placement, etc.). Jordan's still digging into this, so treat the post template as a rough starting shape for now rather than something to polish — more to come once the content side is sorted.
How does the hero hold up without real photos yet?
Shop photos are thin right now and the old hero isn't usable. Worth thinking through how it looks on stock or a placeholder until real shots come in.
Blog: real posts at launch, or structure only?
Brand new section with nothing to inherit from the old site. A take on how a near-empty blog should look and feel would help, whichever way the content ends up going.
How prominent should the certification badges be?
BRP Master Technician and Lakeview Gold Dealer status are strong trust signals. If actual badge graphics show up, how much visual weight should they carry versus staying text-only for now?
