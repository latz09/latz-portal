Project Snapshot

The job

For 25 years, Music Therapy Services of Central Wisconsin has earned real trust with families across the region — but the site representing them online is a locked-down Turbify build from the year they opened, one that can't even be pinch-zoomed on a phone. It's also trying to do three jobs at once and doing none of them well: convince an anxious parent this practice is the right fit, give a referring professional a fast and simple way to send a patient their way, and convince a music therapist to join a team that's carrying a long waitlist. Winning looks like a homepage that speaks clearly to families and prospective therapists, a Get Started page that works just as well for a case manager submitting a referral, and a CMS simple enough that Elizabeth and clinic staff can run it themselves.

Client & Audience

The Client
Who They AreMusic therapy clinic (LLC), women-owned, Stevens Point WI — 25+ years serving Central Wisconsin.
Our ContactElizabeth, Social Media Coordinator — friendly project champion, but not the decision-maker.
Who Updates SiteElizabeth + clinic staff, via the custom CMS — team roster, job listings, service list, basic copy.
ToneProfessional, clinical, evidence-based — but warm, colorful, and kid-friendly. Not cold or clinical-only.
The Audience
PrimaryThree distinct groups: parents/caregivers seeking care, referring professionals (social workers, case managers, other providers) sending a patient their way, and music therapists considering a job.
What They NeedParents need fast reassurance and a clear way to start. Referring professionals need a quick, no-friction way to submit a referral. Therapists need culture, openings, and a reason to apply.
DevicesMobile-heavy — parents searching on phones. Current site doesn't even support pinch-zoom.
Key InsightThere's no single "typical visitor" — three different people land on this site with three different goals, and Get Started alone has to serve both parents and referring professionals.
💡
Vibe in one sentence Fun, colorful, and kid-friendly — without losing an ounce of clinical credibility. Board-certified expertise wrapped in a site that feels warm, not sterile.
🤝
Working with Elizabeth Elizabeth is easy to work with — responsive, organized, and a genuine advocate for this project. But she's upfront that anything touching scope, budget, or the service list has to go through Richelle, the owner, before it's final. Keep what you hand back simple enough that Elizabeth can walk it to Richelle on her own — she's presenting this, not just relaying it.

Let's Talk

☕
Here's what I know so far — let's figure out the rest together None of this is locked in. Take a look, sit with it, and let's talk through where you land — I'd rather hear your read on these than hand you a checklist to fill out.
Open Design Questions
What does the brightened blue actually look like?
The plan is to keep the logo and base blue once files come in from the client — but the brighter, kid-friendly version is wide open. Curious what direction you'd take it.
How does the dual-audience fork read visually on the homepage?
Two clear paths — families and prospective therapists — need to feel distinct without splitting the brand in two. What's the visual device that makes that fork obvious at a glance?
How deep does Careers go?
Options range from a simple "we're hiring" post to full on-site applications with resume upload. Your instinct on how much this page should carry would help settle it.
Real photos or a considered stand-in?
Photos of children need parental permission before they can go live. Worth deciding now whether the design leans on illustration, pattern, or color in the meantime rather than getting stuck waiting on permissions.
Where does the "As Seen On" news feature live?
A local news segment is a strong trust signal for parents. Once the link comes in, where does it sit — Who We Are, or somewhere more prominent?
