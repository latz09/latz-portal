A Note From Jordan

Stevens Point, WI
Meg, Mary, and Alison,

The restored first floor of your museum may be the best reason anyone has to visit, and right now your website doesn't show a single room of it. Meanwhile, Google quietly sends researchers to more than thirty years of Artifacts issues sitting on that same site, traffic you never paid for. The site is doing one job by accident and missing the one you'd most like it to do.

That's what this proposal is built around. The wireframe I sent shows the site page by page: a homepage that looks like the museum people already follow on Facebook, a Visit page that leads with the house, events Alison can add in minutes, and a way to join or give by card, online and at the front desk.

I know Monday is the first time the board is hearing this, so I've kept it plain and built it so every piece stands on its own. The website is the project. Professional photography isn't part of this price at all; I've noted where it fits and can point you to someone local. You'll also see the things we haven't settled yet, named up front, because I'd rather you know than be surprised.

Thank you for the invitation to the Christmas Tree Walk. I'd like to walk through the house before we design anything. And if anyone on the board has a question before Monday, email me directly. You'll get me, not a ticket number.

Why This Site Matters

Think about how someone decides to visit a place they've never been. Before they drive over, before they tell a friend, before they sign up for a tour, they look it up. Usually on a phone.

Right now, that look-up shows them a site that dates to around 2008, a menu in small type, and no photo of the restored first floor, which may be the best reason there is to visit.

You don't have to use the web for the web to matter to the museum. The teacher planning a field trip, the family new to town, and the researcher tracing a great-grandparent all use it before they ever use the front door. A new site doesn't change what the museum is. It lets people see the house room by room before they arrive, and it gives the ones who already love it an easy way to join, give, and show up.

None of this asks your curator to become a web person. It's built so an event goes up in a few minutes and the rest takes care of itself.

Jordan

It protects what already works

More than thirty years of Artifacts issues sit on your site as PDFs, full of family names, businesses, and local history. Google sends researchers to them, and none of that traffic was ever paid for. In a careless rebuild those files move or vanish, and the traffic stops and doesn't come back on its own. Here, every issue keeps its exact web address or is forwarded one by one to its new home. That's included in the base price, because it's a condition of rebuilding, not a bonus.

It makes joining and giving easy

Today a card payment means a check in the mail or a PayPal link. The new site takes cards online and at the front desk through one system, so someone standing in the house with a card in hand can join or buy a ticket on the spot.

It stays easy to keep fresh

A site only works if it's current. Your curator adds an event in a few minutes, flags it as featured, and the homepage updates on its own. If a site goes stale again, it didn't do its job, so everything here is built around keeping that from happening.

You own it, start to finish

No template. No monthly fee to us. No license that lapses. The site, the CMS, the hosting account, and the analytics are yours, and when you need help, you talk to me directly and pay only for the work you ask for.

We'll fine-tune it together until it's ready to launch. That includes testing on phones and computers, trying every form and payment, and checking that old web addresses still land in the right place. Your curator gets walked through the CMS so she's comfortable before launch day.

The site goes live on your domain, coordinated with Solaris and only after staff email is mapped, so nothing breaks. Your sitemap is submitted and Search Console is set up. And you get the keys to your CMS, your hosting, and your own analytics. The site is yours to edit, monitor, and grow, forever.

About 17 weeks, and April 1 is the finish line

From the board vote to opening day is about 17 weeks. That date is firm, so a few things need to line up on schedule:

  • The board's decision. The earlier we know which pieces you want, the more room we have.
  • Access from Solaris in December. Your domain and DNS, plus a clear picture of how staff email works. Nothing about your web address or email changes until that's worked through with Solaris first.
  • Your logo and brand settled by about mid-January. Whoever creates it, and however it's quoted, design can't finish without it.
  • The seven pages confirmed, with a short list of what moves over and what links out.
