Discovery Questions

🎯
Notes to self, not a script Meg answered the basics, so these build on her answers instead of re-asking. The priority is getting enough to quote by Nov 30: budget, migration scope, and what the board needs to see. Everything else can be rounded out after the quote goes in.
Quote, Budget & Board Approval (do first)
What does the board need from the quote: one number, or options?
She needs it for the 2027 budget at the Nov 30 meeting. If you can give a phased option (logo and core site first, archive migration later), the board has something to approve even if the full number is a stretch. Ask what format they've used for past vendor proposals.
Is there a budget range the board already has in mind for this?
Frame it as helping you right-size the quote so it lands in a range they can actually approve. They're self-funded with no government operating money, so there's probably a number or at least a ceiling. If there's nothing, ask what they spent on past projects or what the current site cost.
Who presents it on Nov 30, and does the board vote on it that night?
Meg is Secretary. Find out if she's presenting, who else has a say, and whether approval is a simple vote or goes through a committee. Also whether the budget approval covers the whole project or just a first phase.
What happens if you need to work from a scoping call and not a full scope?
You'll probably quote off one conversation. Set expectations that the quote is built from what you know, with clear assumptions listed, and that it can be refined after approval.
Goals & the Current Site
What specifically is hard for the curator, and what's hard for the public?
She said "not user friendly for curator and public" but not why. Curator side is likely editing: how do they add events, exhibits, or archive items now, and where does it break? Public side: finding things, joining, giving. Get one or two concrete frustrations for each.
Who's the curator, and who else updates the site?
This is where the custom CMS fits: simple for a volunteer or curator, no monthly fees, they own it. Find out how tech comfortable they are and what happens when the current person steps back.
Who built the site, where's it hosted, and who owns the domain?
You've seen the site, so skip "what do you have." Go straight to access. Volunteer-run nonprofits often lose track of who controls the domain and hosting. Needed before launch, and before April 1 could slip.
How much of the current site needs to carry over?
The scope driver. Digital Library, 30+ years of Artifacts back issues, interactive maps, oral histories, publications. Find out what's actively used and what can be archived or dropped. This is the difference between a clean mid-size site and a big migration. Don't finalize a number until you know.
Membership & Donations
How do people join and give today, and what's working?
She said members and donors are the goal, so this is the conversion funnel. Find out what handles payments now, how many members they have, how renewals work, and where people drop off. They're also on Benevity per their May 2026 newsletter, so ask if that stays.
Anything else that needs to be transactional?
Event tickets, gift shop, publications and books they sell. Affects integrations and scope.
Brand & Logo
Do you have a logo now, and how attached is the board to it?
Didn't find a distinct one on the site. She said the refresh includes a new logo, but ask whether the current mark is worth keeping any elements of, and whether the board is ready to let it go.
How do you feel about building the identity around the Witter house?
The 1907 mansion ("Shadowlawn") is their biggest asset and an obvious anchor. Find out if they want to lean on the building, the T.B. Scott Library history, or something else, and what's off-limits. Also ask for any logos or sites they love, other museums included.
Where will the logo live?
Website, exhibit signage, membership materials, newsletters, Facebook, merch. Determines whether Alyssa needs a simplified mark and alternate lockups. April 1 exhibit opening may mean signage or print needs.
Branding Kit & SEO (added Nov 13)
What do you want in the branding kit?
She asked for an "updated branding kit" with no detail. Ask where they'd use it: signage, exhibit materials, membership and donation materials, newsletters, Facebook, merch. This decides which playbook tier fits. A rebrand with a new logo, guidelines, and templates points to Brand Identity. Pricing isn't final, so check with Alyssa before you quote.
Who will use the kit day to day?
Volunteers and the curator are making newsletters (Adobe Express), flyers, and social posts. Simple guidelines and ready-to-use templates matter more to them than a long brand book.
What brand assets exist right now?
Logos, colors, fonts, signage, printed pieces, photos, and what's on the Facebook page. Tells you whether this is cleanup or a fresh start.
When someone searches for the museum or local history, who do you want to reach?
Visitors, members, donors, genealogists and researchers? Ties SEO back to her stated goals of members and donations. SEO Foundations covers titles and descriptions, link-sharing data, structured data, sitemap, and Search Console after launch.
What does "SEO help" mean to her?
Make sure she's expecting the foundation built into the site. Keyword and content strategy is not part of SEO Foundations, and ongoing SEO monitoring isn't something to offer yet. Don't promise rankings. If she wants more than the foundation, scope it separately.
Does anyone manage Search Console, analytics, or a Google Business Profile today?
You need access, and the Digital Library and Artifacts pages likely have search traffic you don't want to lose in the migration. Plan redirects for the old URLs.
One combined quote, or itemized for the board?
Logo, branding kit, website, and SEO as one package is simpler to approve, but itemized lets the board fund phases if the full number is too high. Decide with Alyssa before Nov 30.

They publish monthly newsletters through Adobe Express, so someone handles content. Settle the copy and SEO overlap before you quote.
