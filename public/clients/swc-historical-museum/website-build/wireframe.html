Museum Website Rebuild · Phase 1

South Wood County Historical Museum

A new website for the 1907 Witter home, built around events, membership and the stories the building already tells.

This wireframe walks through the six pages planned for the April 1 season opening: a homepage that looks like the museum people already follow on Facebook, a Visit page that shows off the restored first floor, events Alison can add in minutes, membership and giving with card payments, the newsletter posted on the site as it comes out, a simple way to get in touch, and a short collections page that keeps the Artifacts back issues right where they are and lets people browse them. Each page shows what content goes on it and how it works. A few more pages are mapped out for after launch, further down. The look comes later, with the new brand.

Phase 2
The searchable archive, after launch

The Artifacts back issues, and why they matter more than they look. Thirty-plus years of Artifacts issues sit on the current site as PDFs, full of family names, businesses and local history. That is how Google sends researchers to you today, and none of that traffic was ever paid for. Two things protect it. Every issue keeps the exact web address it has now, or is forwarded one by one to its new one, so nothing that works today stops working. And a simple Collections page goes up at launch so those issues can still be browsed, and so the rest of the site still points at them. The fuller searchable archive comes later.

Replacing a site from around 2008. Everything on the current site gets a new home here, or a clear reason it doesn't. The about and history story now lives inside Visit & The Building rather than on a page of its own. The two pages marked for later are planned for, and the site is built so each one can be added when you're ready, without starting over.

The web address and staff email. Solaris holds swch-museum.com, while staff email runs on southwoodcountyhistory.org. How those two connect isn't confirmed yet, so nothing about the web address or the email changes until that's worked through with Solaris first.

⚑ A choice to make: the payment system
Square. Takes cards on the site, and a small reader handles the front desk.
Stripe. Takes cards on the site, with a reader option for the front desk.
PayPal. Already part of how you take payments today, and it can cover the front desk too.
The goal is one system covering both the website and the front desk, so someone standing in the house with a card in their hand can join or buy a ticket right there. All three can do that. On your transaction sizes the fee difference across all 285 members works out to roughly $25 a year, so fees aren't really what this turns on. The real question is which one is easiest for Alison and the volunteers to run. We've built with all three, and we'll confirm the current rates in writing before you decide.
⚑ A choice to make: Benevity
Keep it. Stays as one more way for people to give.
Drop it. Everything runs through the one payment system instead.
It was used once, for the spring tea, and nobody used it.

Each monthly issue is also posted on the site like a short article, and the old blog goes away. One place now does the job of both. Alison publishes a new issue in a few minutes. Issues have run monthly since March 2025, and every one of them lives here.
⚑ A choice to make later: how the newsletter gets emailed
Posting works from day one. Writing an issue and putting it on the site needs nothing outside the website itself.
Emailing it out is a separate piece. The tool that sends it today goes away with the old site, so a replacement gets chosen, the subscriber list moves across, and the web address gets set up so the mail actually reaches inboxes.
That last part depends on sorting out the web address and email with Solaris, so it is deliberately kept off the April 1 path. Nothing about publishing on the site waits for it. Issues are designed in Adobe Express today, which can carry on or move onto the site, and that is part of the same later conversation.
