1

Do You Need Fully Custom Software?

Short answer: probably not all of it. Here's why.

The Scope You Asked Owen For Is Big

POS, meal inventory, ingredient inventory, recipe costing, vendor payments, and production scheduling, all integrated — that's not a small feature request, it's a full food-service operations platform. That's the same category of software built by companies like Toast, MarketMan, Craftable, and Apicbase, whose entire business is building and maintaining exactly this. That's not a knock on Owen or anyone else you talk to — it's just worth knowing there's a whole market of proven, purpose-built tools for this before comparing what a from-scratch build would cost against them.

🛠️ Worth Asking In Your Meetings

One thing worth asking anyone quoting you custom software: will it be documented? Established platforms come with real help centers, guides, and support — you can look something up at 9pm and get an answer without waiting on a person. A custom build is only as good as what's left behind for you once it's finished. It's worth asking directly: "Will you document how this works? Will I have clear, written instructions for running this day to day, even if you're not available?" The answer tells you a lot about how this will feel two months after launch, not just on launch day.

2

Ordering & Production Tools

Software built specifically for businesses shaped like yours already exists.

GoPrep (and a few alternatives) Existing Software

GoPrep is built specifically for meal-prep and subscription meal businesses — online ordering with weekly/monthly subscriptions, automated production reports (what to prepare and how much), packing slips, delivery route optimization, nutrition and allergen labeling, and payment processing. It covers a surprising amount of what you described to Owen — the POS, some of the inventory planning, and the production-scheduling piece — already built, for a monthly fee instead of a custom build.

How It Would Connect To Your Site

Your website itself would still be fully custom and fully yours — your story, your photos, your gifting page, all of it. The one difference is the ordering flow: instead of everything happening inside your own site's code, an "Order Now" button hands the customer off to a GoPrep-hosted ordering page. GoPrep does offer custom domain masking and custom branding, so that page can carry your colors and logo and live under your own domain rather than looking like a random third-party tool — but it is a hand-off moment, not a seamless single app. Worth knowing plainly rather than discovering it later.

Recipe Costing & Ingredients

None of the above touches this — it's a separate piece either way.

The Gap GoPrep Leaves Open

GoPrep's "inventory" is really an ingredient shopping list for production planning — it tells you how much to order so you don't run short. It doesn't touch what things actually cost: no unit costs, no supplier or vendor management, no cost-per-meal or margin math. That's genuinely a different problem, and it's worth solving separately regardless of which path you take on the ordering side.

On Me Building It

Before web design, I spent years as an executive sous chef — I know recipe costing, unit conversions, and yields from the kitchen side, which is usually the part that trips up software built by people who've never had to do it by hand. I'm not going to try to out-build the tools above; the goal would be something dead simple: enter an ingredient and its cost, build a recipe from ingredients, see the cost per serving. If I build it, it gets documented the same way everything else here does — plain instructions for running it yourself.

4

A Fully Custom Path

If a hand-off moment doesn't sit right with you, there's a way around it.

Square's Subscription API Fully Custom

If you'd rather nobody ever leaves your site at all, this is the way to get there. Square's Subscriptions, Catalog, Orders, and Customers tools let me build the entire ordering and subscription experience myself, directly on your site — Square just handles billing and payment quietly in the background. No redirect, no "you're now somewhere else" moment, fully your brand start to finish.

The trade-off: Square only handles the commerce side. It does nothing for production planning or kitchen operations, so that piece — the part GoPrep gives you for free — would need to be custom-built too. It's a real option, just a bigger scope commitment than pointing at existing software.
