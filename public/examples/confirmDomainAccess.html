GoDaddy — Delegate Access

GoDaddy calls this "Delegate Access." It's built for exactly this.

  1. Go to your GoDaddy Delegate Access page (Account Settings → Delegate Access).
  2. Under "People who can access my account," click Invite to Access.
  3. Enter my name and email when prompted.
    NameJordan Latz
    Emailjordan@latzwebdesign.com
  4. Select access level: Domains Only (not "Products and Domains").
  5. Click Invite — I'll get an email to accept.
Note: "Domains Only" keeps this scoped to DNS and nameservers — I won't see billing, payment info, or anything else on the account.

Namecheap — Share Access

Namecheap calls this "Share Access," set per domain.

  1. Go to Domain List → click Manage next to irishhospitalapartments.com.
  2. Open the Sharing & Transfer tab.
  3. In the Share Access section, enter my email as the New Manager.
    Emailjordan@latzwebdesign.com
  4. Check the Advanced DNS permission box.
  5. Enter your Namecheap password to confirm, then click Confirm.
Note: This is set per domain, not account-wide — it only applies to irishhospitalapartments.com.

Squarespace Domains — Domain Manager

Squarespace treats domain permissions separately from website permissions.

  1. Open your Domains dashboard, then click irishhospitalapartments.com.
  2. Click Permissions.
  3. Click Invite Domain Manager.
  4. Enter my name and email when prompted.
    NameJordan Latz
    Emailjordan@latzwebdesign.com
  5. Click Submit.
Don't see "Invite Domain Manager"? That means your domain shares permissions with a Squarespace website. In that case, go to that site's Settings → Permissions and add me as an Administrator instead.

Bluehost — Accounts & Users

Role-based access through the Bluehost Portal.

  1. Log in and click your profile icon (top right) → Accounts & Users.
  2. Find your account, click Manage.
  3. Scroll to User Roles & Permissions, click + Add User.
  4. Enter my name and email, and choose the Tech role.
    NameJordan Latz
    Emailjordan@latzwebdesign.com
  5. Click Invite.
Note: "Tech" is the right scope for DNS work — it skips billing and account-level settings.
