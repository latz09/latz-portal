Discovery Questions

🎯
Notes to self — not a script You don't need all of these on the first callback. Goal of the call is to understand what's driving this and book the real meeting. These are the things to work through by the time you've had the full discovery conversation. Skip anything they've already answered.
Business & Goals
What's got you thinking about updating the site now?
The single most important question. Is it the look, the fact that they can't change it themselves, mobile, the monthly cost, or something specific (rebrand, new services, recruiting push)? Everything else scopes off this answer.
What's not working about the current site?
Let them vent. On Turbify the usual complaints are "we can't update it without calling someone," "it looks dated," "it's clunky on phones." Their answers tell you what to lead the proposal with.
Who's the site really for — and what do you want it to do?
Families/caregivers looking for a therapist? Referring providers (OT/PT/speech, schools, hospices, nursing homes)? Prospective hires? They're actively recruiting, so the site may be doing double duty as a careers tool. Success might be "more inquiries," "easier for families to get started," or "look as credible as we are."
Content & Structure
Keep the same page structure, or rethink it?
They've got a solid set already — About, Services, Team, Get Started, Contact, Careers. Most of it can carry over. Worth asking if anything's missing (testimonials, insurance/FAQ, specific programs, blog for SEO).
How does a new client currently get started — and does that flow need to change?
They have a "Get Started" page and a Fusion Web Clinic portal. Understand the intake path (form? call? referral?) so the new site makes that easier, not harder. The portal itself stays external.
Who's got the content and photos — and who'll write anything new?
Most copy already exists on the current site, so migration is realistic. But if they want fresh photography or new service pages, someone has to produce that. Clarify whether that's on them or something you're scoping in.
Design & Brand
Do you have a logo and brand colors you're keeping?
Confirm what carries over vs. what's up for grabs. If the brand feels dated to them, there may be a logo/refresh conversation too — but don't assume it; ask.
Any sites — therapy practices or otherwise — whose look you like?
Gives Alyssa direction and saves revision cycles. For a therapy practice the tension is usually warm/human vs. clinical/credible — worth hearing which way they lean.
What impression should someone get in the first five seconds?
Trustworthy and established? Warm and family-friendly? Evidence-based and professional? They're all three — but which leads shapes the whole design.
Technical & Practical
Who owns the mtscw.com domain, and where's it registered?
On Turbify the domain is sometimes tangled up with their hosting/builder account. You'll need to confirm they can point it wherever you host. Flag early — it can be the fiddliest part of a Turbify migration.
Are you in a contract with Turbify, and what are you paying them monthly?
Two birds: it tells you what they can exit and when, and it hands you the "no monthly fees, you own it forever" contrast on a plate — using their own number.
Any forms or integrations that need to work — contact, referral, intake?
Confirm the Fusion Web Clinic portal just gets linked. Ask about a referral form (providers refer into practices like this a lot) and whether scheduling@mtscw.com email stays as-is.
Do you want to be able to update the site yourselves after launch?
This is the whole pitch. Elizabeth runs social — she's probably itching to update team members, events, and content without filing a ticket. Your custom CMS is the answer. Don't oversell it, just note they'll be able to.
Decision-Making, Timeline & Budget
Besides you, who else weighs in on this — and who signs off?
Elizabeth is likely the champion, not the final yes. Find out if Richelle/ownership needs to be in the discovery meeting. Nothing stalls a project like designing to one person's taste and then meeting the real decision-maker at proposal time.
Is there a timeline or anything driving a deadline?
A rebrand, a grant, a recruiting season, an annual Turbify renewal they'd love to cancel? Even a soft "before fall" tells you how to pace it.
Do you have a budget range in mind?
Frame as scoping, not a gotcha: "That helps me figure out the right level of build and what makes sense for the deliverables." If they don't have a number, give a range once you understand scope — you've already seen the current site, so you're close.
Looking Ahead
Anything else changing on the marketing side — social, branding, SEO?
Elizabeth owns social, so there may be an appetite to tie the site into a broader refresh. Opens the door to ongoing SEO/copy work if they want it, and makes sure the site isn't designed in a vacuum.
04

If They Ask You

💬
In case they have questions right away The point of the callback is to book the real meeting — but be ready to field these without getting pinned down. Keep it easy and confident, don't quote a firm price blind, and steer toward "let's sit down and I'll show you exactly what makes sense."
Likely Questions → Your Answers
"How much does a website cost?"
Don't quote blind. "It depends on the scope — that's exactly what I'd want to nail down when we sit down. Once I understand what you need, I'll give you a clear, flat number — no surprises." If pressed for a ballpark, give a range and tie it to scope.
"Do you charge monthly fees?"
This is your home run. "No monthly fees. You own the site outright — forever. No being locked into a builder or paying rent on your own website." Straight contrast with Turbify.
"Can we update it ourselves?"
"Yes. I build a custom CMS that's clean and simple — no clutter — so your team can update pages, staff, and content without calling anyone. I'll walk you through it." Perfect for Elizabeth, who runs their social.
"How long does it take?"
"Depends on scope and how ready the content is — but I'll give you a real timeline once I've seen everything. I've already had a look at your current site, so I'm not starting from zero." Don't commit to a hard date on the phone.
"Can you keep our patient portal?"
"Absolutely — your Fusion portal stays exactly where it is, we just link to it cleanly from the new site. Nothing changes on that side for your patients."
"Will we lose our Google ranking?"
"No — I handle that with proper redirects and on-page SEO so you keep what you've built. I actually saw you're targeting Stevens Point, Wausau, Rapids, and Marshfield — we can strengthen that, not lose it."
"Are you local?"
"Right here in Stevens Point — happy to meet in person whenever works for you. That's kind of the point: when you need something, you talk to me directly, not a ticket system."
"What do you need from us to get going?"
"Not much to start — just a conversation about what you want. Down the line, your content, photos, logo, and access to your domain. I'll guide the whole migration; you won't have to figure out the technical stuff."
Callback Arc — Keep It Light
1. Thanks for the voicemail — glad she reached out. Warm, quick. 2. "So you're thinking about updating the site — tell me what's got you looking into it?" Then listen. Let her tell you what's frustrating them. 3. Nod to a couple of specifics so she knows you already looked: you've seen the current site, you're local in Stevens Point, you know they've been at this since '99. 4. Propose the real meeting: "The best thing would be to sit down for 20–30 minutes — I can come to you — so I can see everything and give you something concrete. Or a call if that's easier. Whatever works." He's local, so in-person is the move. 5. If she asks the money/monthly/DIY questions — answer from the section above, stay easy, don't get pinned to a number. 6. Close: get her email, confirm who else should be in the meeting (Richelle?), lock a day. Low pressure the whole way.
